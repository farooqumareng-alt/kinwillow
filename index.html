How KinWillow works

Three steps, about an hour, at your pace.

1

Build your vault

A guided checklist walks you through accounts, subscriptions, documents, and devices — where each thing is, how billing works, and where password recovery lives. Never the passwords themselves.

2

Name your people

Choose trusted contacts and set exactly what each person can see, and when. In an emergency, they can request access — you're notified, and nothing is released instantly.

3

Stay current automatically

Renewal and expiry reminders keep the vault from going stale, and built-in guides walk you through switching on Apple, Google, and Meta legacy settings.

What KinWillow is — and is not

Clear about its job, so your family can rely on it.

KinWillow is

  • The map that makes a will, an executor, and a grieving family actually able to act
  • A living inventory that stays current with renewal reminders
  • Instructions in plain English: cancel these, keep these photos, call this person first
  • Shared only with the trusted contacts you name

KinWillow is not

  • A will — and it is not legal advice
  • A password manager — it never stores your passwords
  • Something your family has to figure out alone in a crisis
